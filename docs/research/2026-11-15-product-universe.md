## 1. Summary

1. **Direct plans cost less, and the gap is mostly the distributor's trail commission.** Typical direct-vs-regular gap: about 0.5–1.0 point a year for active equity, 0.2–0.6 for debt, near zero for index funds (Practitioner, **Medium**, no AMFI table found). On a ₹10,000 monthly SIP at 11% gross, a 1-point gap costs **₹1.10 L over 10 years (5.2% of the pot) and ₹9.2 L over 20 years (11.2%)**; a 0.5-point gap costs ₹0.56 L and ₹4.7 L (Calc).
2. **SEBI's new Mutual Funds Regulations, 2026 (in force 1 Apr 2026) renamed the cap the "Base Expense Ratio" and trimmed it by about 10–15 bps.** Total cost = BER + brokerage + regulatory levies + statutory levies, shown separately. Brokerage cap is 6 bps (cash) and 2 bps (derivatives); the extra 5 bps tied to exit loads was dropped. So the *headline* TER a family sees is not directly comparable with pre-April 2026 numbers. The app should store TER as reported and never hard-code a cap (Regulator, High: Reg 66–67 of the Regulations read; the 10–15 bps saving is from news).
3. **A regular plan is easy to spot:** the scheme name says "Regular", and the CAS shows an ARN code (or a sub-broker code) against the folio where a direct holding shows "Direct" (Practitioner, Medium). Switching regular→direct is a **sale**, so it is taxed. **Switching usually pays back in 3–6 years, not never**, because the tax is only brought forward (the basis resets). Example in §2.4: ₹10 L, ₹4 L gain, 1-point gap: ahead by ₹15k after 3 years, ₹34k after 5 (Calc). The best time is when the sale gain fits inside the member's unused ₹1.25 L LTCG exemption (TAX).
4. **Active large-cap funds lose to the index far more often than not in India (S&P SPIVA Year-End 2025, read on S&P's page).** Underperformance rates for large cap: 75.0% / 74.2% / 84.4% / 76.3% over 1/3/5/10 years (benchmark S&P India LargeMidCap). ELSS: 69.2% / 55.0% / 58.5% / 82.9%. **Mid/small cap was the exception in 2025** (12.1% underperformed in the year, the best since 2014) but 41.5% / 46.0% / 79.0% over 3/5/10 years. A firm majority in every category lagged over the decade; 27% of all funds in the sample died (merged or liquidated) in 10 years. Source: Regulator-grade primary for S&P (Institution, **High**). Caveat: one benchmark per category; large cap is measured against a large+mid index.
5. **Academic work agrees on the direction but not on size.** IIMB thesis (251 funds, 1995–2021, survivorship-free): weak outperformance that "disappeared after 2005", no significant 1-year persistence. Other Indian studies find no net abnormal return and only short-horizon persistence (Paper, Medium–High, abstract level). So the default is **index** for large and large+mid cap, with active allowed in mid/small cap only where the member wants it.
6. **Within a tax type the category choice matters as much as the tax.** For parking money under 12 months, arbitrage beats liquid only for holders above roughly the 20% slab (20.8% STCG with cess against slab tax), and for holds above 12 months above about the 13% rate (Calc, §4.1). Equity savings, balanced advantage ("Dynamic Asset Allocation" since SEBI's Feb 2026 revision) and arbitrage are **equity-taxed**. Conservative hybrid is **slab-taxed**. Balanced hybrid (40–60% equity) falls in the middle "other" bucket.
7. **International funds are a supply problem.** The RBI/SEBI overseas limits ($7 B industry, $1 B per AMC, $1 B for ETFs) have been hit; many AMCs stopped fresh money in 2026 (Practitioner, Medium; Value Research counted 54 of 66 closed at some date). The app must not default a family to an international fund it cannot buy. Make it a "if open" slot.
8. **Small savings rates for Oct–Dec 2026 are unchanged:** PPF 7.1%, SSY 8.2%, SCSS 8.2%, NSC 7.7%, KVP 7.5%, MIS 7.4%, 5-year RD 6.7% (DEA OM F.No.1/4/2019-NS dated 30.09.2026, read via a bank page and news; Regulator via secondary, **Medium**). EPF is 8.25% for FY 2025-26; FY 2026-27 is not yet declared.
9. **Tax-free interest is worth more to higher-slab members; for nil-tax members it is worth nothing extra.** PPF 7.1% tax-free equals 10.3% taxable at a 31.2% marginal rate, but only 7.1% for a nil-tax parent, where a 7% debt fund or a bank FD ties it. EPF/VPF at 8.25% (tax-free on employee contributions up to ₹2.5 L a year) is the best risk-free return available to a salaried member at any slab, and beats any debt fund even for a nil-tax parent.
10. **NPS is the one deduction that survives in the new regime** (employer share, 14% of salary, s.124(2), TAX §3.6). Rules changed in Oct–Dec 2025: 100% equity possible via the Multiple Scheme Framework, and non-government subscribers may take up to 80% as a lump sum at normal exit (Regulator via secondary, **Medium**; PFRDA text not read). NPS sits between a debt fund and a hybrid in tax type and is locked until 60.

### 2.3 Spotting a regular plan (for the family)

1. **Scheme name** in the CAS or statement ends in "Regular Plan" or has no "Direct" in it. Direct ones say "Direct Plan" (or "Direct").
2. **Distributor field.** In a CAMS/KFintech CAS, each folio shows the ARN (distributor code, like ARN-12345) or "Direct". A sub-broker code or "INA/INH" for advisers can also show. An ARN means a commission is being paid.
3. **TER pair.** The factsheet lists TER for both plans; regular is higher.
4. **NAV.** The two plans of one scheme have different NAVs, direct higher.

The app should read plan type from the CAS or statement import and mark each holding `plan: direct | regular | unknown`. If `unknown`, ask. Evidence: Practitioner, **Medium** (CAS layout from memory; check with a real CAS file).

### 2.4 Tax cost of switching, and a break-even rule

Switching regular→direct is a redemption plus a fresh purchase. Consequences:

- Gain is realised now. Equity-type: STCG 20% if held under 12 months, LTCG 12.5% above the member's ₹1.25 L yearly exemption (TAX). Slab-rate funds (debt, "specified"): gain taxed at slab regardless of holding. Other types: per TAX (and see §4.3 on holding period, unconfirmed).
- Exit load applies if within the load period (usually 1% within 12 months for equity funds; varies).
- STT on equity redemption (0.001% for equity-oriented units, from memory, unconfirmed) is small.
- Basis resets to the sale value. Tax is **deferred, not avoided**, if you do not switch; so the "tax cost" of switching is only the time value of paying early plus the loss of the old basis when the old holding was later going to be sold at a lower rate. For equity LTCG the tax cost is small.
- Switching the whole holding can use the member's free ₹1.25 L LTCG exemption. A family with several members can shift in slices over years, each within the exemption.

Calc: ₹10 L regular holding, basis ₹6 L, LTCG at 13% (12.5% + cess), 11% pre-cost return, terminal sale taxed at 13% on gain:

| Years held after | Gap 0.5 pt: switch minus stay | Gap 1.0 pt: switch minus stay |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | −₹625 | +₹15,237 |
| 5 | +₹1,730 | +₹33,865 |
| 7 | +₹7,505 | +₹62,190 |
| 10 | +₹25,831 | +₹1,30,525 |
| 15 | +₹1,01,344 | +₹3,57,172 |

(Tax paid today on the switch: ₹52,000; no exemption assumed.) So with a 0.5-point gap it takes about 3–5 years, with a 1-point gap well under 3 years, to break even after tax if the gain is long-term. If the gain is short-term (20.8%), or an exit load applies, wait.

**Break-even method (pseudocode).**

```
function switch_decision(h, member, plan_direct, horizon_years, r):
    # h: holding (value V, cost basis B, tax type, acquired date, exit load rule)
    gap  = TER(h.fund, regular) - TER(direct twin of h.fund)         # points a year
    if no direct twin or gap <= 0.1pt: return KEEP("no real gap")
    gain = V - B
    load = exit_load(h, today) * V
    stt  = 0.00001 * V if equity_oriented else 0                       # unconfirmed rate
    # tax today on realising the gain
    if h.tax_type == EQUITY:
        free = remaining_ltcg_exemption(member)                       # 1.25 L less used
        rate = (12.5% if held>12m else 20%) * (1 + cess)
        tax_now = rate * max(0, gain - (free if held>12m else 0))
    elif h.tax_type in (SLAB, SPECIFIED): tax_now = slab_marginal(member) * gain
    else: tax_now = per_type_rate(h, member) * gain                   # see TAX
    cost_now = tax_now + load + stt
    # terminal values after-tax at the horizon, same pre-cost return r
    stay   = V * (1 + r - gap)^n ; stay_at   = stay   - T(stay - B)
    switch = (V - cost_now) * (1 + r)^n ; switch_at = switch - T(switch - (V - cost_now))
    # T = tax function at horizon with the member's expected rate (use the "cheapest holder" at sale)
    edge = switch_at - stay_at
    n* = smallest n in 1..40 with edge(n) >= 0   # break-even years
    if gain_is_short_term and months_to_12 <= 6 and load == 0: return WAIT(until=12m)
    if load > 0 and months_to_load_free <= 6:           return WAIT(until=load_free)
    if n* <= min(horizon_years, 7) and edge(horizon) >= max(₹5,000, 0.5% of V): return SWITCH
    if any other member holds unused exemption and the holding is jointly owned: return SWITCH_SLICES
    return KEEP
```

Tests: (a) gap 0 → KEEP; (b) LTCG fully inside the exemption → `cost_now = load + stt`, n* < 1; (c) STCG with 3 months to go → WAIT; (d) slab-rate fund, 30% slab, gap 0.3 pt, 3-year horizon → KEEP; (e) nil-tax parent holding a debt fund → switching costs nothing in tax, always SWITCH if gap ≥ 0.1; (f) switch edge is monotonic in gap and in horizon.

## 4. Category choice within a tax type

Tax types follow the app's engine (TAX §3.9). SEBI's categorisation circular (6 Oct 2017, SEBI/HO/IMD/DF3/CIR/P/2017/114, inside the Master Circular for Mutual Funds) was revised on **26 Feb 2026** for hybrids (clause 2.6). I read the revision through Upstox/ELP [S18], [S19], not the SEBI text.

### 4.1 Equity type (≥65% domestic equity, including arbitrage)

| Category | Rule (SEBI) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Arbitrage | ≥65% equity and equity-related, arbitrage strategy; debt only G-secs under 1 year and repo; no InvITs | Short-term parking by high-slab members. **Equity-taxed**. |
| Equity savings | ≥65% equity (gross, includes arbitrage), ≥10% debt, net equity 15–40% (Feb 2026), disclose max arbitrage and hedged/unhedged | 1–3 year money for a member who wants a little equity risk. Equity-taxed. |
| Balanced advantage / Dynamic asset allocation | Equity and debt only, managed dynamically | 3–5 year goals, low behaviour risk. Equity-taxed **if** the fund holds ≥65% in equity including hedged positions (check the scheme). |
| Aggressive hybrid | 65–80% equity | Equity type; long-term lower volatility. |
| Equity (large, mid, small, flexi, multi, index, ELSS, sector, thematic) | per SEBI categories | Equity type. |

**Arbitrage vs liquid (Calc).** Assume pre-cost returns are equal (`r_a = r_l`; in practice within about 0.5 point, unconfirmed). After-tax: liquid `r (1 − t)`; arbitrage held under 12 months `r (1 − 0.208)`; held over 12 months `r (1 − 0.13)` (before the ₹1.25 L exemption).

| Holder slab (with 4% cess) | Liquid after tax per 7% | Arbitrage under 12 m | Arbitrage over 12 m | Better |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nil | 7.00% | 5.54% | 6.09% | Liquid |
| 10% (10.4%) | 6.27% | 5.54% | 6.09% | Liquid |
| 15% (15.6%) | 5.91% | 5.54% | 6.09% | Liquid under 12 m; arbitrage over 12 m |
| 20% (20.8%) | 5.54% | 5.54% | 6.09% | Tie under 12 m; arbitrage over 12 m |
| 30% (31.2%) | 4.82% | 5.54% | 6.09% | **Arbitrage** |

Rule: arbitrage for money parked ≥ 12 months at a slab of 15%+; for under 12 months only at 25%+ (arbitrage can show a slightly lower pre-tax return when spreads are thin, so ties go to liquid, an Opinion). For emergency money use liquid or overnight: the arbitrage NAV can dip for days and redemption is T+2 for some funds (unconfirmed; DEBT E6). A nil-tax member always takes liquid, money market or a bank deposit.

**Conservative hybrid vs balanced vs equity savings.** Under the post-Apr 2025 "specified mutual fund" definition (more than 65% in debt and money market instruments, unconfirmed against the Act), conservative hybrid (10–25% equity) is **slab-taxed**. Equity savings is equity-taxed, so a 30% member gets 12.5% on a similar risk profile: an equity-savings fund beats a conservative hybrid on tax for a high-slab holder over 12 months; for a nil-tax parent, conservative hybrid is fine (no tax difference, more debt yield). Balanced hybrid (40–60% equity, no arbitrage) is neither: it lands in the "other" bucket (see §4.3).

### 4.2 Slab-rate type (specified MF, bank deposits, interest)

Specified debt funds: tax at slab on any holding period (s.76, TAX). Choose by horizon and credit risk, not by tax.

| Horizon | Default category | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund (DEBT) | Overnight, liquid (up to 91 days), money market (up to 1 year) | Credit risk |
| 1–3 years | Short duration, banking & PSU, corporate bond (80% AA+ or better), target-maturity index | Long duration, credit risk |
| 5+ years | Gilt, target-maturity (held to maturity), or PPF/EPF where available | – |
| Under 1 year for high-slab members | Arbitrage (§4.1) | – |

SEBI definitions are Medium confidence (from the 2017 circular, not re-read).

**EPF/VPF break-even (Calc).** For a member whose employee contribution already exceeds ₹2.5 L (including the mandatory 12%) VPF stops being tax-free; the extra interest is taxed at slab, so 8.25% × (1 − 0.312) = 5.7% at the 30% slab, below PPF 7.1% and similar to a taxed bank FD. Under ₹2.5 L: 8.25% tax-free beats every debt product at every slab, including a 7% debt fund held by a nil-tax parent. Rate is declared yearly and has no guarantee (risk: the rate can fall).

**NPS exit and rules (PFRDA amendments notified Dec 2025, secondary readings [S24–S26], Medium):**

- Normal exit (60+): non-government subscribers may take up to **80% lump sum** and must annuitise at least **20%** (was 60/40); small corpus bands (no annuity if total ≤ ₹8 L; ₹8–12 L lump sum cap about ₹6 L, unconfirmed). Government employees keep 60:40.
- Exit age can be deferred to 85.
- Premature exit (before 60 or before 15 years): at least 80% annuitised (sources differ: one says up to 20% lump sum above ₹5 L).
- Tax on the NPS lump sum and annuity (annuity taxed at slab; lump sum exempt up to a limit) is **unconfirmed** under the 2025 Act; leave to the CA question.
- Costs: fund management charge historically 0.03%–0.09% a year; a Mint report says a tenfold rise to as much as 0.3% came with the MSF (unconfirmed). Account charges small. Cost is still below most mutual funds. Asset allocation: Auto choice (lifecycle) or Active (E/C/G/A caps: equity up to 75%, or 100% in the new MSF scheme).

## 7. Design rules for the app

1. **Store `plan: direct | regular | unknown` on every holding.** Show the rupee cost of a regular plan over the member's horizon. Offer the switch card only when the break-even rule (§2.4) says SWITCH or SWITCH_SLICES.
2. **Run switches through the harvesting engine.** Treat the ₹1.25 L LTCG exemption as free room to move holdings to direct plans, member by member, each year.
3. **Defaults are categories, not funds.** The app says "a Nifty 50 index fund, direct plan, with TER under 0.3%", never a scheme name. Show a "what to check" list: TER, 3-year tracking difference, AUM, plan.
4. **Index default for large cap and broad equity; mid/small flagged "less evidence".** Never rank by past return.
5. **Short-term parking uses the slab-vs-arbitrage rule** (§4.1) and the member's actual slab, not a general rule.
6. **ELSS appears only for old-regime members with unused 80C room.** For others, show a plain equity fund.
7. **International slot is conditional on an open scheme** and capped at 10–15% of equity.
8. **Small savings rates come from a quarterly data file.** Re-read the DEA OM every quarter. Flag a stale rate after 100 days.
9. **EPF/VPF and PPF rank above debt funds for members where the tax-free rate wins** (§5.2). Nil-tax parents: SCSS and bank FD first.
10. **NPS employer share is the new-regime lever**; NPS own contributions only for old-regime members.
11. **Every product row carries `confidence`.** Lower-confidence rows are shown with a "check this" note in plan reports.

## 8. Regulatory cautions and open questions

### 8.1 Cautions

1. **No scheme names, AMC names or "best fund" lists** while the founder is an RIA (and before). Name the index only. Product recommendation by category is fine (generic, with the criteria).
2. **Direct plans only** in recommendations (U8); no distribution income, no referral fees from AMCs, platforms or insurers; the family of the founder cannot distribute to clients (DEBT R2–R3).
3. **Small savings and NPS** are not SEBI products. State that in the plan disclosure (DEBT R2). Do not route users to a PFRDA point of presence or a post office for commission.
4. **SPIVA and other studies are statements about past funds.** Word the explanation as "most funds in this category have lagged their index", not "index funds will win".
5. **International funds:** do not promise availability; the limits can change on RBI/SEBI notice.
