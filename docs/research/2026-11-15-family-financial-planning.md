# Family financial planning in India: process, tax nuances, placement (FY 2026-27)

Research note, 15 Nov 2026. Tax year 2026-27 under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force 1 April 2026), as amended by the Finance Act, 2026. New section numbers come first, old (1961 Act) numbers in brackets.

**How this was checked.** Section text was read on the Income Tax Department's own section pages (incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-…) and in the department's PDF of the Act as amended by the Finance Act 2026. Some section pages show the 2025 enacted text, not the 2026 amended text. For those, the limits were cross-checked against the amended PDF (sections 19, 22, 109, 110, 123, 124, 126, 129, 134, 153, 156 and 202 matched). Sections 196, 198, 404 and 425 were read in their 2026 versions. Anything not confirmed in a primary source is marked **(unconfirmed)** and listed in "Open questions".

## 1. Summary for the product: the 10 nuances that matter most

Ranked by how much after-tax money they move for a typical 5-member family, and how often they apply.

1. **The new-regime ₹12 L rebate makes a no-income member a ₹12 L shelter for slab-rate income.** Interest, debt-fund gains and rent in the hands of a parent or adult child with no other income pay nil tax up to ₹12 L total income (s.156(2), old 87A). For a 30%-slab earner the same income costs 31.2%.
2. **The rebate does not cover special-rate capital gains, and those gains count toward the ₹12 L test** (s.156(3), s.198(7)). One extra rupee of equity gains over ₹12 L total income can cost tens of thousands. The app must model this cliff.
3. **A resident with income below the basic exemption can use the gap against STCG and LTCG** (s.196(2), s.198(3)). In the new regime a member with no other income can realise about ₹5.25 L of equity LTCG (₹4 L gap + ₹1.25 L exemption) or ₹4 L of STCG at nil tax each year.
4. **Gifts to parents, siblings and adult children are not clubbed; gifts to spouse, son's wife and minor children are** (s.99, old s.64). This rule decides whether moving an asset to a member actually moves the tax.
5. **Old vs new regime per member.** For a salaried member, the old regime only wins with more than about ₹8 L of deductions beyond the ₹50,000 standard deduction at incomes over about ₹25 L, and about ₹5.4 L–₹7.25 L at ₹12.75 L–₹20 L. Most families will have most members on the new regime. Usually only a member with a home loan, high HRA and full 80C/80D use is a candidate for the old regime.
6. **Chapter VIII deductions (80C, 80D, NPS) cannot reduce special-rate capital gains** (s.196(4), s.198(6)). A member whose income is mostly equity gains gets little from 80C-type products.
7. **House property loss: ₹2 L set-off against other heads (including capital gains) in the old regime only.** The balance is carried forward 8 years against house property income alone (s.109(1)(b), s.110). In the new regime the loss cannot be set off at all and is lost (s.202(2)(b)(ii)).
8. **Capital losses only offset capital gains.** Short-term losses offset any gain. Long-term losses offset only long-term gains. Both carry forward 8 years (s.108(2), s.111). No other head can absorb them (s.109(2)).
9. **Senior parents (60+) with no business income pay no advance tax** (s.403(3), old 207). They get ₹50,000 of deposit interest deducted (s.153, old 80TTB) in the old regime only. They can file Form 121 (replaces 15G/15H) to avoid TDS when their tax is nil. This makes parents the natural holders of FDs, SCSS and debt funds.
10. **Equity LTCG can be fully exempted by buying one house** (s.86, old 54F, ₹10 Cr cap). 54EC bonds (s.85) and s.82 (old 54) do **not** apply to equity. This matters only for large, lumpy gains.

## 2. The planning process (question 1)

### 2.1 The standard process

FPSB (the global body that licenses the CFP mark, and FPSB India locally) defines a six-step process ([FPSB](https://www.fpsb.org/financial-planning-process/); [FPSB India standards](https://india.fpsb.org/standards)):

1. Establish and define the relationship with the client.
2. Collect the client's information (goals, cash flow, assets, debts, insurance, tax, family).
3. Analyse and assess the client's financial status.
4. Develop the recommendations and present them.
5. Implement the recommendations.
6. Review the client's situation over time.

SEBI's rules for registered investment advisers (RIAs) add hard requirements around steps 2 to 4:

- **Risk profiling comes before any advice.** The RIA must assess the client's risk profile using income, age, market experience and similar facts. Advice may only follow once the client has consented to the completed profile, by registered email or on paper ([SEBI IA FAQ, Aug 2025, Q36](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/aug-2025/1755174193178.pdf); IA Regulations 2013, Regs 16–17).
- **Suitability.** Advice must fit the risk profile, and there must be a documented process for selecting products (Reg 17).
- **No free trials before risk profiling.** SEBI's October 2024 circular bars them.
- **"Family" for an individual client** means the dependent spouse, dependent children and dependent parents ([SEBI IA FAQ Q31](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/aug-2025/1755174193178.pdf)). That is narrower than the app's "family" (adult earning children, a working spouse). It matters for how fees and the client relationship are scoped.
- **Implementation only through direct plans**, and the RIA takes no commission (FAQ Q34).
- **Tax advice from a CA incidental to tax work is exempt from RIA registration** (FAQ Q20). Investment advice as a business is not.

### 2.2 The order of work in practice

Indian planners (CFP and NISM X-A/X-B curriculum) work through these areas in a fixed order. Each one is a precondition for the next, because it protects the plan from a shock that would force selling at a bad time.

| # | Area | What is done | Rule of thumb in use | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cash flow and budget | Household income and expenses per member; surplus available to invest | Savings rate of 20–30% of take-home is "healthy" | Convention. No regulator sets it. |
| 2 | Emergency fund | Liquid money for job loss or medical shocks | 3–6 months of expenses; 6–12 months for single-income or self-employed families | Convention taught in NISM and CFP material. Not a regulation. |
| 3 | Insurance: life | Term cover for each earner with dependants | Cover = 10–15× annual income, or Human Life Value (present value of future income for dependants), or needs-based (debts + goals + expenses − assets) | HLV and needs methods are textbook methods. "10–15×" is a convention. |
| 3 | Insurance: health | Family floater plus separate cover for senior parents | ₹10 L+ per family in metros; top-up or super top-up for big events; separate policy for parents | Convention. Industry practice. |
| 4 | Debt | List loans by rate and tax benefit; prepay vs invest | Prepay anything above ~10–12% (cards, personal loans) first. For a home loan, compare the **post-tax** loan rate with the expected post-tax return. Total EMIs ≤ 40–50% of take-home. | The EMI cap is a bank underwriting norm (FOIR), not law. The prepay rule is arithmetic. |
| 5 | Goals | Children's education, marriage, house, retirement; inflate each goal; set a target date | Education inflation 8–10%; general 5–6%; retirement corpus 25–33× first-year expenses | Inflation figures are planner assumptions. The 25–33× corpus comes from 3–4% safe-withdrawal studies (US data), not from Indian research. **Convention only.** |
| 6 | Risk profile and asset allocation | Questionnaire per member; target equity/debt/gold split per goal | Equity % ≈ 100 − age; equity only for goals 5–7+ years away; 5–10% gold | **Convention only.** SEBI requires a risk profile, but not any particular split. |
| 7 | Tax planning | Regime per member; use deductions; place assets in the right member's name; harvest | See sections 3 and 4 below | Law |
| 8 | Estate | Nominations on every folio, demat, bank account and policy; a will; guardianship for minors | Nominee ≠ legal heir. A will is needed in any case. | Law (succession acts; SEBI nomination rules). Not researched in depth here. |
| 9 | Review | Yearly, and on life events | Rebalance when off target by ±5 percentage points | Convention |

**Where this app differs.** Planners treat tax as step 7. The app's thesis makes tax the lens through every step. Examples: an emergency fund held as a debt fund in a nil-tax parent's name; insurance premiums paid by the member on the old regime; home loan co-borrowers chosen so both claim interest.

## 4. What changes placement decisions (question 3)

### 4.1 Which nuances move "who holds what" and "where new money goes"

| Nuance | Effect on placement |
|---|---|
| New-regime ₹12 L rebate | Put **slab-taxed income** (FDs, debt funds, SCSS interest, rent, bonds) with members whose other income is low. Each member can absorb up to ₹12 L of total income at nil tax. |
| Rebate excludes 196/198 gains, but they count toward ₹12 L | Do **not** stack equity gains on a member sitting near ₹12 L of slab income. Move the gains to another member, or spread them over years. |
| Basic exemption gap (residents) | A member with no other income can realise ₹4 L + ₹1.25 L of equity gains at nil tax each year in the new regime. Put **equity to harvest** with non-earning adult members. |
| ₹1.25 L LTCG exemption per person | Spread equity across all adult members, and an HUF if any, so that each harvests ₹1.25 L a year |
| Clubbing | Gifts to spouse or minor do not move the tax. Gifts to parents and adult children do. Use a market-rate loan for a spouse. |
| Old regime only (80C, 80D, 24(b), HRA, 80TTB) | Deduction-linked products (ELSS, PPF lock-ins, health premium, home loan) only help members who stay on the old regime. For new-regime members, judge each product purely on post-tax return. |
| s.124(2) employer NPS 14% | The only deduction a new-regime salaried member can grow: ask the employer to restructure pay |
| House property loss (old only) | A let-out house with a big loan belongs with an old-regime owner who can use the ₹2 L set-off. In the new regime the loss is lost. |
| Senior: no advance tax, Form 121 | Parents can hold interest-paying assets without cash-flow friction (no TDS, no advance tax) |
| Debt fund (s.76) taxed at slab | Its holding period no longer matters. **Who** holds it matters a lot: 0% vs 31.2%. |
| Equity in an old-regime member's name | Chapter VIII deductions cannot shelter 196/198 gains, so the old regime adds nothing for gains |

### 4.2 Worked examples

All figures include 4% cess and exclude surcharge. Computed with the FY 2026-27 slabs above.

**Example 1. A debt fund: 30%-slab earner vs a retired parent on the new regime.**
- ₹10 L in a debt fund earning 7% = ₹70,000 a year of slab-taxed gain.
- In the name of a salaried member with ₹30 L income: tax = ₹70,000 × 31.2% = **₹21,840 a year**.
- Gifted to his mother, aged 66, on the new regime, with ₹3 L of pension: her total income = ₹3.7 L. That is under ₹12 L, so the rebate covers it. Tax = **₹0**.
- The gift is exempt (mother is a "relative", s.92). There is no clubbing (a parent is not in s.99).
- Saving: ₹21,840 a year. Over 10 years with growth, more than ₹2.5 L.
- Caveat: the money is legally hers. It passes under her will, so the will and nominee checklist matters.

**Example 2. Harvesting equity in a no-income member's name.**
- Equity mutual fund units with ₹5.25 L of unrealised LTCG.
- Sold by the 30% earner: tax = (₹5.25 L − ₹1.25 L) × 12.5% × 1.04 = **₹52,000**.
- If the units had been gifted earlier to an adult daughter with no income, on the new regime: her other income is ₹0, so the ₹4 L nil band is unused. s.198(3) cuts the gain by ₹4 L. The remaining ₹1.25 L is within the exemption. Tax = **₹0**.
- Re-buying the units resets her cost basis upward.
- Same for a father on the old regime aged 70: nil band ₹3 L, so ₹4.25 L of LTCG is tax-free. At 80+: ₹6.25 L.
- Gifting units off-market costs stamp duty (0.015%) and a DP charge. The engine already models these.
- Cost and holding period carry over to the receiver on a gift (old s.49 rule). **New-Act section not confirmed.**

**Example 3. The ₹12 L cliff when gains are stacked on interest income.**
- A parent, new regime, with ₹11 L of FD and debt interest.
- Realising ₹1 L of LTCG: total income ₹12 L. Slab tax on ₹11 L (₹50,000) is wiped out by the rebate. The LTCG is within ₹1.25 L. Tax = **₹0**.
- Realising ₹2 L of LTCG instead: total income ₹13 L, so the full rebate goes. Marginal relief gives nothing here, because the tax (₹50,000 + ₹9,375 = ₹59,375) is below the ₹1 L excess. Tax ≈ **₹61,750**.
- An extra ₹1 L of gains cost about ₹61,750, an effective 62%.
- Rule for the app: when a member's total income is near ₹12 L, move further gains to another member or into another year.

**Example 4. A home-loan member choosing a regime.**
- Salaried, ₹25 L gross. Deductions available: 80C ₹1.5 L (EPF + principal), 1B ₹50,000, 80D ₹25,000, self-occupied home loan interest ₹2 L.
- New regime tax: **₹3,19,800**.
- Old regime with those ₹4.25 L of deductions: **₹4,36,800**. Old regime with ₹3 L of HRA exemption as well: ₹3,43,200. Both still lose.
- So this member stays on the new regime, and the home loan interest gives **no** tax benefit.
- Effect on prepay vs invest: the loan's after-tax cost is the full rate (say 8.5%). Prepaying is a risk-free 8.5% return. It beats a debt fund held by this member (8.5% vs about 7% × (1 − 31.2%) ≈ 4.8%). It does **not** beat that same debt fund held by the nil-tax mother from Example 1 (7% tax-free) by much.
- If the house were jointly owned with a spouse who also earns, each co-borrower could claim ₹2 L. That only helps if one of them is on the old regime.

**Example 5. Senior parent: old or new regime for FD income.**
- Father, 65, ₹9 L of FD interest, no pension.
- Old regime: ₹9 L − ₹50,000 (s.153) = ₹8.5 L taxable. Tax = 5% on ₹3–5 L + 20% on ₹5–8.5 L = ₹80,000 → **₹83,200**.
- New regime: ₹9 L ≤ ₹12 L, so the rebate applies. Tax = **₹0**.
- The ₹50,000 senior interest deduction is worth little next to the new regime's ₹12 L rebate. The app should default seniors with interest-heavy income to the new regime.
- The old regime helps only a senior with large 80D or medical claims and income above ₹12 L.

## 6. Open questions and unconfirmed figures

1. **TDS threshold on interest for seniors (₹1 L) and others (₹50,000)** under the new s.393 table: not read in the Act text. Confirmed only for FY 2025-26 under old s.194A (Finance Act 2025).
2. **Rules 2026 figures**: children's education allowance ₹3,000/month and hostel ₹9,000/month; the HRA 50% city list adding Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. From BDO and Mondaq summaries; the CBDT notification itself was not read. One secondary source gave ₹1,000 for education allowance.
3. **Life insurance maturity thresholds** (₹5 L non-ULIP, ₹2.5 L ULIP, 10% of sum assured): carried from the 1961 Act. Exact Sch II text not read.
4. **EPF interest threshold** (₹2.5 L / ₹5 L): located in Sch II / Sch XI by search; text not read.
5. **SCSS deposit cap** (₹30 L) and **small savings rates for Oct–Dec 2026** (PPF 7.1%, SSY 8.2%, SCSS 8.2%, NSC 7.7%): from news reports of the 30 Sep 2026 notification. The Department of Economic Affairs notification was not read.
6. **Sovereign Gold Bond exemption** limited to original subscribers who hold to maturity: Budget 2026 reports only.
7. **Specified mutual fund (s.76, old 50AA)** text not read. The mapping was supplied in the brief.
8. **Cost and holding period on gifted assets** (old s.49(1), s.2(42A) rules): the new-Act location was not found.
9. **Choice of set-off order** for house property loss against special-rate capital gains versus slab income: the Act allows set-off against capital gains, but whether the taxpayer may choose to apply it to the 12.5%/20% gains first was not confirmed.
10. **Rebate test on "income"**: whether special-rate gains count toward the ₹12 L limit in s.156(2)(a). The common reading (used in Example 3) is yes. A Sept 2026 BCAJ article calls the related question of rebate against special-rate gains "controversial". Treat it as a risk.
11. **Joint home loan split**: standard practice, but no new-Act CBDT guidance found.
12. **The loan route to a spouse**: an interpretation of "adequate consideration", not confirmed by a circular.
13. **80DDB, 80DD, 80U and 80G new section numbers** were not checked.
14. **SEBI fee caps per family** for RIAs: not researched. Relevant for the registration plan.
15. **Several department section pages show the 2025 enacted text.** Limits were cross-checked against the amended PDF. A Finance Act 2026 change not caught by that check is possible for sections 82, 85, 86, 92, 99, 108, 111 and 403.
