# Debt, emergency fund and insurance in the plan

Companion notes: **TAX** (`2026-11-15-family-financial-planning.md`), **DATA** (`2026-11-15-india-returns-risk-inflation.md`), **PLACE** (`2026-11-15-family-asset-placement.md`), **GOAL** (`2026-11-15-goal-based-planning.md`).

## 1. Summary

1. **A loan prepayment is a debt investment with a certain return equal to the loan's after-tax rate.** So it competes with the family's *debt* sleeve, not with equity. The right comparison is the loan's marginal after-tax rate against the best after-tax debt return available to **any eligible family member** (the "cheapest holder"), not against the borrower's own.
2. **For most home loans the tax break does not change the answer.** A member on the new regime gets no interest deduction (TAX §3.2). In the old regime the ₹2 L cap means that on a loan above roughly ₹24 L at 8.5%, the *marginal* rupee of interest is not deductible at all, so prepaying costs no tax benefit. The deduction matters only for small or late-stage home loans and for education loans (s.129, uncapped, 8 years, old regime only).
3. **RBI has removed prepayment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes**, in part or in full, with no lock-in, for loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 Jan 2026 (Regulator, High). The app can therefore treat prepaying a floating home, education or personal loan as free of charges; fixed-rate loans still need the lender's charge as an input.
4. **"Reduce tenure" and "reduce EMI" earn the same return** on the money prepaid (the loan rate). Tenure reduction looks like it "saves more interest" only because it keeps paying the higher EMI. The app should choose by cash-flow resilience (EMI-to-income, emergency fund), not by interest saved.
5. **Debt payoff order: avalanche on after-tax rate, one loan at a time.** Papers show people prefer closing small accounts (Amar et al. 2011) and that closing accounts predicts finishing (Gal & McShane 2012). The cost of snowball is usually small but can be large: in this note's credit-card case it costs ₹35,772 (44%) more interest. Show the rupee cost and let the family choose.
6. **Emergency fund: 3 months of essential outflows is the floor; 4–12 months by a points rule** (single income, variable or business income, dependants, uninsured members). The convention comes from NISM/CFP teaching, not regulation. Survey evidence supports *some* cash buffer strongly (Fed SHED, JPMorgan Chase Institute, Vanguard); evidence for any exact number of months is weak.
7. **Hold the emergency fund in three layers** (instant / 1 day / a few days) and, where the person is trusted and the gift is acceptable, in the name of the lowest-slab adult who is not the spouse (spouse income is clubbed back). Liquid and debt fund gains are taxed at slab, so in a nil-tax parent's name they are tax-free; for a 30% member they lose 31.2% of the return.
8. **Insurance is a set of gap checks, not product advice.** Term cover: needs-based method first, Human Life Value as the upper bound, 10–15× income only as a fallback. Health cover: city-tier floor with super top-up, parents separate, employer cover counted as temporary. 80D (s.126) helps only members on the old regime.
9. **IRDAI line:** an unregistered app must not solicit, generate leads, compare or rate products, or take any payment from insurers (Insurance Web Aggregators Regulations 2017, Sch IV and XI). Generic "you appear under-insured by about ₹X" is fine; naming, ranking or linking to products is not. A SEBI RIA may include insurance in a financial plan with a disclosure that it is outside SEBI's purview, and must not distribute (SEBI IA Reg 22).

## 3. Recommended methods

Common inputs (already in the foundation member model or questionnaire): per member — regime, marginal slab rate incl. cess `t_m`, distance to the ₹12 L rebate cliff, age, income type (salaried-stable, salaried-variable, business/self-employed, pension, none), take-home; per loan — type, borrower(s) and shares, balance, rate, fixed/floating, benchmark (repo/MCLR), remaining months, EMI, prepayment charge %, tax treatment (self-occupied / let-out / education with first-repayment year / none), purpose (business or not); household — essential monthly spend excluding EMIs, city tier, dependants.

### 3.1 Marginal after-tax loan rate `r_eff(L, P)`

For prepayment `P` on loan `L` with rate `r`, compute the year-1 interest before (`I0`) and after (`I1`) prepayment from the amortisation schedule, then the deduction lost:

```
for each borrower b with share s_b:
  if L.type == home_self_occupied and regime(b) == old:
      cap_b = 2_00_000 − other self-occupied interest claimed by b
      lost_b = min(s_b·I0, cap_b) − min(s_b·I1, cap_b)
  elif L.type == education and regime(b) == old and year within s.129 window:
      lost_b = s_b·(I0 − I1)
  elif L.type == home_let_out:
      lost_b = value of the change in let-out loss usable this year
               (old regime: within ₹2 L set-off + carry-forward value discounted 1 yr;
                new regime: only to the extent interest ≤ rent of that property)
  else: lost_b = 0
  tax_lost += lost_b · t_b
r_eff = (I0 − I1 − tax_lost) / P
```

Tests: new regime → `r_eff == r`. Old regime, ₹50 L at 8.5% → `r_eff == r` (cap binds both before and after). Old regime, ₹20 L at 8.5%, 30% slab → `r_eff ≈ 5.85%`. Education loan in year 9 → `r_eff == r`. Co-borrowers each get their own cap.

### 3.2 Best after-tax debt return in the family `r_debt*`

`r_debt* = max over eligible holders h of r_debt_pre · (1 − t_h_marginal_for_debt)` where eligible = the borrower; the borrower's parents, adult children, HUF (gift route, not clubbed); the spouse **only for the spouse's own money** (gift income is clubbed back). Use PLACE §3 headroom: a holder's marginal rate includes the ₹12 L cliff (if the extra interest crosses ₹12 L, use the effective cliff rate, TAX ex. 3). Default `r_debt_pre` = base-case short debt fund from DATA §3 (≈7%). If no gift is acceptable to the family (questionnaire flag `gifts_ok = false`), only the borrower and spouse-own-money are eligible.

### 3.3 Prepay-vs-invest decision (per spare lump sum or monthly surplus)

Order of operations; stop at the first rule that takes the money:

1. **Emergency fund below target (3.6)** → fill it first. Never prepay from the emergency fund.
2. **Any loan with `r_eff ≥ 12%`** (cards, most personal loans, consumer loans) → pay it, in avalanche order (3.5).
3. **Goals within 3 years** that the plan funds from debt → keep their money; prepaying makes it illiquid.
4. **For each remaining loan, margin `m = r_eff − r_debt* − charge_amortised`**, where `charge_amortised = charge% / min(remaining years, 5)`:
   - `m ≥ +0.5 pt` → prepay is the debt sleeve's best home: **recommend prepay** up to the debt-sleeve amount the allocation already calls for.
   - `−0.5 < m < +0.5` → **neutral**; default to keep liquid (invest in the debt sleeve), mention prepay as equivalent.
   - `m ≤ −0.5` → **invest** the debt sleeve with the cheapest holder.
5. **Equity sleeve:** prepaying never takes money the risk profile assigns to equity, unless the family sets a "debt-free by <date>" goal (then it is a goal in the GOAL waterfall).

Output per loan: `r_eff`, `r_debt*` and its holder, margin in points, rupee gain over 5 years `= P·((1+r_eff)^5 − (1+r_debt*)^5)`, the decision and a one-line reason ("Your home loan costs 8.5% after tax; the best safe return for the family is 7.0% in Mother's name; prepaying ₹5 L is ₹48k better over 5 years").

**Fallback (no member tax data):** compare loan rate (minus 30% only for an old-regime education loan) with 7% for debt; prepay if loan ≥ 8%, invest if ≤ 6.5%, neutral between.

Tests: (a) monotonic in `r` — raising the loan rate never flips "prepay" to "invest"; (b) adding a nil-tax parent can only move the decision toward invest; (c) emergency fund short → never prepay; (d) the ₹12 L cliff: a holder near ₹12 L is not chosen when the extra interest pushes them over; (e) fixed-rate loan with 2% charge and 1 year left → charge dominates.

### 3.4 Tenure versus EMI on prepayment

Both options earn exactly `r` on the prepaid money. Choose by resilience:

```
FOIR_after_emi_cut = total_EMI_after / household_take_home
choose "reduce EMI" if any:
   FOIR (current) > 40%
   emergency fund < target
   any earner's income type is business/variable
   rate-shock FOIR at +2 pts (3.7) > 50%
else choose "reduce tenure"   # default: keeps the forced saving
```

Show both: months saved and nominal interest saved (tenure), EMI drop (EMI), with the note that the difference in "interest saved" is the cost of the extra EMIs, not a better return. Test: for any inputs, IRR of the prepayment under both options equals `r` to 4 decimals.

### 3.5 Debt payoff order

```
pay all minimums/EMIs
extra = surplus allocated to debt by 3.3
key(loan) = r_eff(loan, small P) + charge_amortised penalty
order = sort by key descending   # avalanche on after-tax rate
tie (keys within 1 pt): smaller balance first   # free "small win"
put all extra on order[0] until closed, then roll its EMI + extra to the next
```

Also compute the snowball schedule (balance ascending). If snowball interest − avalanche interest (after tax) ≤ max(₹5,000, 2% of avalanche interest), offer snowball as "same cost, quicker first win". Otherwise show "Paying the smallest loan first costs ₹X more". Progress display: per-loan % repaid and loans closed (D12, D13).

Never in the queue: home loan when `r_eff ≤ r_debt*` (it is handled by 3.3); education loan during moratorium if the old-regime borrower will claim s.129 (flag "pay the moratorium interest if you can": interest is simple during moratorium and an IBA-scheme concession of 1 point may apply).

Tests: avalanche total after-tax interest ≤ snowball for every random portfolio (property test, 1,000 cases); all-equal rates → identical to snowball; 0% loan is always last unless its promo ends sooner (model promo end as a rate change).

### 3.6 Emergency fund size

```
M = essential monthly spend (exclude discretionary) + all EMIs + insurance premiums/12 + school fees/12
months = 4
  −1 if ≥2 earners and the smaller take-home alone ≥ 50% of M
  +2 if one earner provides ≥ 80% of take-home
  +1 if any earner's pay is >30% variable (bonus/commission)
  +4 if the main earner is business/self-employed (replaces the +1)
  +1 if dependants (children + dependent parents) ≥ 3
  +1 if any member has no health cover (see also 3.9)
  +1 if the main earner is ≥ 50 or in a cyclical sector (self-declared)
months = clamp(months, 3, 12)
retired household: months = 6, applied to M − guaranteed monthly income (pension, annuity, SCSS interest), floor 0;
                   the rest of drawdown liquidity is topic 7's bucket.
target = months · M, rounded up to ₹10,000
```

Defaults when unknown: income type salaried-stable; dependants from member list; M = 60% of household take-home if spend not entered (Opinion).

**Credit lines.** Pre-approved loan against FD, shares or MFs (not credit cards, not personal loans) may stand in for part of layer 3 only: `credit_credit = min(0.5 · available_line, 1 · M)` and only when months ≥ 6 and income is salaried. Layers 1–2 are always cash (E3).

Tests: clamp bounds; adding a dependant never lowers the target; a second earner who can cover half of M lowers it by one month.

### 3.7 Emergency fund placement

| Layer | Size | Where | Whose name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Instant | 1 × M | Savings / auto-sweep account | Any adult who manages the household (accessibility first) |
| 2 One day | 1.5 × M (or rest if smaller) | Liquid or overnight fund (instant-redemption facility covers small draws), or sweep FD | Lowest marginal-rate eligible adult (3.2 rules), who can transact within a day |
| 3 Few days | Remainder | Liquid / money-market fund, short FD, or arbitrage fund for a 30% holder; credit line credit per 3.6 | Same rule |

Tax selection per holder: for slab `t ≤ 5%` → liquid fund (tax at slab on redemption, ≈0); for `t ≥ 20%` with holding > 12 months likely → arbitrage fund (equity taxation; LTCG 12.5% above the member's unused ₹1.25 L) vs liquid at `t`: pick the higher after-tax yield using DATA rates. Never place layer 1 with a member who lives elsewhere or needs help to transact. Check the ₹12 L cliff for the holder (PLACE §3).

Also run a **rate-shock check** with this: EMIs at +1 and +2 points on every floating loan (EBLR resets within 3 months, D8). If FOIR at +2 > 50% → add 1 month to the target and flag.

**Fallback:** 6 × M, one month in savings, the rest in a liquid fund in the lowest-slab adult's own money.

### 3.8 Term life cover check (per earner with dependants)

```
inputs: earner e, take-home T_e, share of household income w_e,
        household essential spend excl. EMIs S, earner's own consumption share c (default 0.15),
        years to dependants' independence n (youngest child to age 22; spouse to 60 if not earning; parents to 85),
        loans where e is borrower B_e (by share), goals funded by e G_e (today's ₹, share w_e),
        investable assets excl. emergency fund A (share w_e), existing personal term cover C_e
        real rate ρ = (1 + r_debt_after_tax)/(1 + CPI) − 1  (default 1.07/1.05 − 1 = 1.9%)
needs = PV_annuity(S·(1−c)·w_e, n, ρ) + B_e + G_e + 2 L (final expenses) − A·w_e
HLV   = PV_growing(T_e·(1−c'), years to 60, g = 6%, d = 7%), c' = 0.25
target = max(needs, 0); ceiling = HLV
gap = target − C_e   (employer group life shown separately, counted 0: it ends with the job)
flag if gap > 10% of target; never flag above HLV
```

Fallback: target = 10 × annual take-home + loans − investable assets.

Outputs: "about ₹X more cover would meet the family's needs" with method shown; no product, insurer, premium quote or link.

### 3.9 Health cover check

```
floor_family = metro: ₹25 L effective (base + super top-up); tier 2: ₹15 L; tier 3/rural: ₹10 L
floor_parents_each_senior = ₹10 L base (+ super top-up to ₹25 L in a metro)
effective = personal base + super top-up (count employer group cover separately, flagged "ends with job")
gap if effective_personal < floor
inflate floors by medical trend (base 10%/yr, DATA) from 2026 when re-checking; re-check every 3 years

80D: show the deduction only for members on the old regime; if one child is on the old regime,

```

Defaults are Practitioner/Opinion (I4); show them as "a common planning floor", editable.

## 4. Worked examples (all ₹, FY 2026-27, base-case rates)

### Example A. Prepay ₹5 L or invest it?

Arjun, 38, salaried ₹25 L, new regime (marginal 31.2%). Home loan ₹50 L, 8.5% floating (repo 5.50% + 3%), 216 months left, EMI ₹45,273. Mother, 64, new regime, total income ₹4 L (nil tax, well below ₹12 L). Gifts acceptable.

- `r_eff` = 8.5% (new regime, no deduction).
- `r_debt*`: Arjun 7% × 0.688 = 4.82%; Mother 7% × 1.0 = **7.0%**. Spouse Meera's gift income would be clubbed to Arjun.
- Margin = 8.5 − 7.0 − 0 = **+1.5 pts** → prepay. 5-year gain ≈ ₹5 L × (1.085⁵ − 1.07⁵) = ₹7.52 L − ₹7.01 L ≈ **₹50,000**.
- Same Arjun on the old regime: year-1 interest ₹4.20 L before, ₹3.76 L after: both above the ₹2 L cap, so no deduction is lost; still 8.5%. Same answer.
- Change the loan to ₹20 L (year-1 interest ≈ ₹1.68 L, under the cap), Arjun on the old regime: `r_eff` = 8.5% × 0.688 = **5.85%** → margin −1.15 → **invest** the debt sleeve in Mother's name.
- Against equity in Arjun's name: 10.2% after tax over 10 years vs certain 8.5%: not compared; equity money stays per the risk profile.

Tenure vs EMI on the ₹5 L: keep EMI → tenure 216 → **172.5 months**, nominal outflow saved ₹14.69 L; keep tenure → EMI ₹45,273 → **₹40,746**, nominal saved ₹4.78 L. Both earn 8.5% on the ₹5 L; the extra "saving" is the extra ₹4,527 a month kept in the loan for 172 months. Arjun's FOIR is under 40% and the emergency fund is full → default **reduce tenure**.

Rate shock: at 9.5% EMI ₹48,396 (+₹3,123); at 10.5% ₹51,611 (+₹6,338).

### Example B. Payoff order with a tax-deductible loan

Extra ₹25,000 a month on top of EMIs (budget ₹60,655). Card ₹60,000 at 42% (₹3,000 minimum); personal loan ₹3 L at 14% (36 m); car ₹4 L at 9.5% (48 m); education loan ₹8 L at 10.5% (96 m) in the name of Meera, on the old regime at 31.2%, in its s.129 window.

| Order | Pre-tax interest | After-tax interest | Debt-free |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avalanche on pre-tax rate (card → PL → edu → car) | ₹2,17,393 | ₹1,78,605 | 30 months |
| **Avalanche on after-tax rate** (edu = 7.22%, so card → PL → car → edu) | ₹2,20,464 | **₹1,72,303** | 30 months |
| Snowball (by balance) | ₹2,20,464 | ₹1,72,303 | 30 months |

The tax lens saves ₹6,302 over pre-tax avalanche, and here it matches snowball, so the app can offer the "small wins" order at no cost.

### Example C. When snowball is expensive

Extra ₹15,000. Card ₹2.5 L at 42% (₹12,500 minimum); 0% appliance EMI ₹40,000 (₹4,000/m); gold loan ₹60,000 at 10% (₹2,000/m); personal loan ₹1.5 L at 15% (24 m).

- Avalanche: interest **₹80,582**, debt-free in 15 months (card closed month 11).
- Snowball: interest **₹1,16,354**, 16 months (card closed last, month 16).
- Snowball costs **₹35,772 (44%) more**. The app shows: "Paying the smallest loans first would cost about ₹35,800 more. We suggest the card first."

(Simulation: monthly interest, minimums first, all surplus to the top of the order, freed EMIs rolled forward.)

### Example D. Emergency fund for Arjun's family

M = ₹80,000 essentials + ₹45,273 EMI + ₹4,000 premiums = **₹1,29,273**. Months: 4 base; −1 (Meera's take-home alone exceeds half of M); +1 (two children + Mother = 3 dependants) = **4 months** → target **₹5.2 L**.

- Layer 1: ₹1.3 L in the joint savings/sweep account.
- Layers 2–3: ₹3.9 L in a liquid fund held by Mother from a documented gift. At 6.5%, tax saved vs Arjun ≈ ₹3.9 L × 6.5% × 31.2% ≈ **₹7,900 a year**; Mother's income stays far below ₹12 L.
- If gifts are not acceptable: layers 2–3 in Meera's own savings (₹11.25 L taxable after standard deduction + ₹25,000 interest stays under ₹12 L → nil tax). Check the cliff yearly.
- Rate shock at +2 points: EMI rises ₹6,338; FOIR stays below 50% → no extra month.

## 6. Regulatory cautions and open questions

### 6.1 IRDAI and SEBI cautions (design rules)

1. **No product names, insurer names, ratings, rankings, quotes, "buy" buttons or affiliate links** for insurance. Show gaps in rupees and the method only. (R1; the Web Aggregator rules bar even registered aggregators from rankings and insurers from paying unapproved comparison sites.)
2. **No lead generation:** do not pass any user details to an insurer, broker or aggregator, and accept no fee, referral or data deal from them (R1, R4).
3. **Generic education is fine** (what term vs endowment means, what a super top-up is, moratorium and portability rules from IRDAI), framed as information, not a recommendation of a product.
4. **When the founder becomes an RIA:** insurance gap checks fall under "financial planning on products not under SEBI's purview": show the disclosure and capture the client declaration (R2). The founder's family must not distribute insurance to advisory clients (R2, R3).
5. **Wording:** "Your cover appears about ₹45 L below a needs-based estimate" — not "buy ₹45 L of term insurance from X".
6. **Loans:** the app likewise must not route users to lenders (RBI digital lending rules on lending service providers; not researched here).

### 6.2 Open questions

1. **Were the 2017 Web Aggregator Regulations replaced** by IRDAI's 2024 consolidated intermediary regulations and master circulars? Read the current IRDAI text before the public launch. If an Account-Aggregator-style feed of policies is used, check IRDAI's Bima Sugam / e-insurance account rules.
2. **Insurance Act s.42 text** and IRDAI's position on non-intermediary apps that *display* a user's own policies (likely fine; confirm).
3. **Bank LAS limit of ₹1 crore**: confirm the RBI amendment direction and effective date.
4. **Liquid fund instant-redemption limit** (₹50,000 / 90%): confirm the current SEBI circular.
5. **Clubbing section numbers in the 2025 Act** for spouse gifts (TAX §3.10 covers the rule; this note cites it by substance).
6. **s.126 cash-payment bar** for premiums under the new Act (old 80D barred cash except preventive check-up): confirm.
7. **Emergency-fund months for Indian households**: no Indian empirical study found; the points rule is Opinion built on convention plus US evidence. Test on the topic 6 archetypes.
8. **Live rate inputs:** bank home/education/personal loan rate cards and FD rates should be fetched, not hard-coded (DATA already flags FD).
9. **Let-out property** prepayment value in the old regime needs the carry-forward rules modelled (s.110, 8 years); the method above approximates it.
