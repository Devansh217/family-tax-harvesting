Family Tax Harvesting

Tax rules, import and backup

How the rules work

Long-term vs short-term. Listed shares and equity mutual funds held more than 12 months are long-term. LTCG is taxed at 12.5% after a ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption per PAN. STCG is taxed at 20%. Add 4% cess, plus surcharge at high incomes.

Gain harvesting. If a PAN has unused exemption, sell long-term winners up to that amount and buy them back. The gain is tax-free and your cost price resets higher, so less gain is taxed later. The buyback starts a new 12-month clock.

Loss harvesting. Selling a loss-making holding books a capital loss. Short-term losses offset short- or long-term gains. Long-term losses offset only long-term gains. Unused losses carry forward 8 years if you file your ITR on time.

Buy back the next trading day. India has no wash-sale rule today. Do not sell and rebuy the same stock on the same day: the exchange nets the two trades into an intraday trade, which is speculative income, not a capital gain or loss. Sell on day T, rebuy on T+1.

Basic exemption shortfall. A resident whose other income is below the basic exemption (₹4 lakh in the new regime) can use the gap against STCG and LTCG. The 87A rebate does not apply to these gains. This is why a parent with little income can take a lot of tax-free gains.

Using another family member's bucket. Gifting shares to a relative is tax-free, and their cost and holding period carry over from you. Income from gifts to your spouse, a minor child, a son's wife or your HUF is clubbed back to you, so those PANs do not help. Gifts to parents, adult children and siblings are not clubbed. Use an off-market transfer with a gift deed. The shares and the sale money then belong to them.

Mandatory set-off. Losses must be set off against gains, including gains that the ₹1.25 lakh exemption would have covered. A carried-forward long-term loss can eat next year's exemption, so booking a loss only helps when there are taxable gains to cancel.

Check before acting. From 1 April 2026 these rules sit in the Income-tax Act, 2025 (old 111A/112A are now sections 196/198). This app is a planning aid, not tax advice. Rates are editable below. Confirm large moves with a CA.
